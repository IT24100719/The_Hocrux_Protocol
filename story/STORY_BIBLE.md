# The Horcrux Protocol: Shadows Over Hogwarts — Story Bible

> Narrative reference for the themed CTFd frontend (Route 1 custom theme).
> This is the story spine the six challenges hang from. Flags and all
> challenge mechanics are unchanged; the story wraps them.
> Fictional fan-narrative, non-commercial, for an isolated university lab.

---

## 0. World premise & the logic of the trail

- **The world.** Years after the war, the magical world built the **Hogwarts
  Research Network** — enchantment fused with computation: portraits as data
  nodes, the **Floo as a protocol**, **Gringotts** as a hardened host, the
  **Restricted Section** as an archive server. That is why Muggle pen-testing
  tools bite on magical systems.
- **The infection.** Tom's contingency seeded the six soul-Horcruxes **into the
  Network's systems** to spread the directive digitally. Each awakened fragment
  is bound to one system, so **getting into the system is how George reaches the
  soul — solving the challenge *is* the sealing.**
- **Investigation *and* aid.** The directive's attempts to execute *are* the
  "suspicious activity" (egress, beacon, intrusion) — real tracks any
  investigator would chase. But the **pure soul, unable to stop the directive,
  smuggles a true message inside its own noise**: the LSB in the map, the
  readable cipher, the one clean TCP stream, the EXIF note. George follows the
  attacker's mess and keeps finding the dead left him a gift inside it.
- **The conductor.** **Dumbledore sets the order.** From the office he routes
  George map → portal → cipher → archive → Floo → vault; each ghost hands him to
  the next because Dumbledore alone sees the whole board. The handovers are his
  routing, not coincidence.
- **Canon note.** "Horcruxes carved into others, unknowingly" is **deliberate
  AU** — canon doesn't permit it. Intentional, for this story.

## 1. Logline

A grieving man who refuses to let the dead stay dead is handed the power to
hoard them — and must instead lay to rest the pure souls of everyone he lost,
one by one, to defuse a dead tyrant's final revenge.

---

## 2. Protagonist — George Weasley

- The twin who **lived**. Fred falls at the Battle of Hogwarts; George carries it.
- Joined the **Auror Office**, then was drawn into the **Department of Mysteries**
  as an **Unspeakable**.
- Haunts the **Time Room**, running unsanctioned loops of the past he can't
  release. The overuse has worn him **half-unstuck from time** — which is why he
  (alone) can perceive the temporal rifts the awakening causes.
- **Arc:** a man who will not let go, given a tool that lets him keep ghosts, who
  must learn that mercy is release — tested by a real temptation to do the
  opposite (see §8).

---

## 3. Antagonist — the Horcrux Protocol (Tom Riddle's last experiment)

- Not Riddle *present*. He spent his **entire soul** in the final battle, but a
  **contingency** fires posthumously.
- Over years, Horcruxes he had carved **in secret into other powerful wizards** —
  without their knowledge, even his own lieutenants — **awaken on their own**,
  carrying one directive: **burn the world that beat him.** His last "gift."
- The cruelty is accidental mercy: the imprisoned **souls themselves are pure**
  (see §4). The weapon is the *directive*, not the people.

---

## 4. The six Horcruxes — the pure dead

Tom bound six souls. They are **truly themselves, uncorrupted** — because
Dumbledore cleaved the weapon-half off each (see §5). As George reaches each one,
that soul **deliberately helps him** (Q8): the dead quietly aiding the living to
defuse the bomb they're trapped in.

| Stage | Domain / artifact | Ghost | Why it fits |
|------|-------------------|-------|-------------|
| **HX-01** | Stego — the Marauder's Map | **Remus Lupin (Moony)** | Map signed Moony/Wormtail/Padfoot/Prongs; hides the first truth for a friend to read |
| **HX-02** | Web — log in **as** `triddle` | **Peter Pettigrew (Wormtail)** | The man who lived twelve years wearing another's face; the impersonation flaw is his |
| **HX-03** | Crypto — Runes, Vigenère key `RIDDLE` | **Severus Snape** | Never what he seemed; leaves a decipherable truth on purpose |
| **HX-04** | Forensics — the Restricted Section archive | **Albus Dumbledore** | Keeper of secrets; the dam; the mid-story confrontation (see §5, §7) |
| **HX-05** | Network — the Floo Network capture | **Sirius Black** | Always spoke through the fire; passes coordinates through the Floo |
| **HX-06** | Linux — the Gringotts vault | **Bellatrix Lestrange** | Canon: the Lestrange vault held a Horcrux. At peace now; wants release |

**Recognition beats (per ghost):**
- **Remus (HX-01).** Died at the Battle of Hogwarts the **same night George lost
  Fred.** The first soul George seals is one of the dead from the night that
  broke him.
- **Pettigrew (HX-02).** "Pure" here is the **guilt-ridden atoner**, not an
  innocent — the traitor finally doing one right thing. His lifelong skill at
  *wearing another's face* becomes the key he hands George (the impersonation
  bypass); George's distrust melting is the beat.
- **Snape (HX-03).** Leaves a truth meant to be broken — "nothing is what it
  seems" is his life and the cipher both. His stage is where the dam first cracks
  (see §8).
- **Sirius (HX-05).** Died through the **Veil in the Department of Mysteries —
  George's own workplace.** He passes the thing that killed Sirius every day.
- **Bellatrix (HX-06).** Molly — George's mother — killed her; she bears George
  no grudge and refuses to go on as Voldemort's leftover. She *wants* out.

## 5. The magic logic (how it all closes)

1. Tom's contingency wires a **directive** into six souls.
2. **Dumbledore**, strong enough to act, performs a **soul-cleaving**: he splits
   each of the six into **[pure soul] + [directive-wraith]**, and pulls every
   wraith — including the **prime wraiths of himself** — onto his **own inner
   battlefield**.
3. Result:
   - The five souls George meets are **pure and willing** (weapon-halves removed).
   - **Dumbledore alone looks "corrupted"** — he holds every directive-wraith
     inside his own soul, fighting them as **ideal Dumbledore vs. the wraiths**.
   - Dumbledore is the **dam**. Every pure soul George seals is one wraith
     Dumbledore can stop shielding. **If the dam breaks before George finishes,
     the directive floods back into whoever is left.** → the ticking clock.
4. George sees these battles through **temporal rifts** (only he can). At HX-04
   he sees them **up close** and learns the cost.

## 6. Key characters, companions & artifacts

- **Fawkes (the Time-Room version).** The phoenix who felt the awakening *first*,
  from inside George's memory loop, and came *through* with him. A phoenix
  slightly out of its own time, companion to a man half out of his. Early-warning,
  courier of Dumbledore's cryptic messages, the only other being who feels the war.
- **The Sanctuary Tool.** A Time Room instrument that lifts a soul *out of the
  directive's timeline* into a stable pocket where it rests at peace (George's
  grief-tool repurposed as mercy). As George leaves the Department after the
  prologue, a **hooded Unspeakable** he does not know presses it into his hands —
  no name, gone before he can ask. **It feels uncannily familiar** — the weight,
  the make, the way it sits in his palm — as though he has held it a thousand
  times, and he cannot say why. That not-knowing nags at him the whole way.
  - *The truth (held for the finale).* It was built in **another branch — the one
    where George died and Fred lived** — and the hooded Unspeakable **is that
    Fred.** On the back, engraved, the **codename only the twins ever knew:
    `GRED & FORGE`** — but George does not register it, or learn who gave him the
    tool, until the **finale**, when the hood comes down and every familiar thing
    finally has a name (see §8). *How* a living Fred crossed over stays a mystery
    (sequel hook).
  - **Sealing is final rest — one-way, irreversible.** That is the mercy; it is
    also why the temptation damns everything (it offers to make the Sanctuary
    *two-way*, for Fred — see §8).
- **The temporal rifts.** Interstitial visions between stages — Dumbledore's war,
  glimpsed. The clock you can see.

## 7. Structure — bookends

- **Prologue (the branch).** George runs an unsanctioned loop: he and Fred hauled
  to **Dumbledore's office** after McGonagall catches their prank — the
  **time-loop corridor**, where (with **Peeves** conducting) anyone who crosses
  relives the same ten seconds, Filch stamping the same three steps over and over.
  They signed it **"Gred & Forge."** (A broken-time joke in the prologue of a
  time-travel story — and the signature that will one day be engraved on Fred's
  tool.) Mid warm conversation, **Fawkes stirs first** — he feels his master's
  soul waking in the present. The disturbance bleeds backward; the loop
  **branches**. Memory-
  Dumbledore *sees* George across time, speaks of **Riddle as a boy** and cryptic
  warnings of dark lords, hands him the **clue to the first Horcrux**, and
  **forces the loop shut** to protect him — with a warning of what is coming:
  *the boy who would be a dark lord never trusted death to keep him; he left the
  world one last gift, and it is waking. The dead you never finished grieving will
  return wearing their own faces. Do not keep them, George — whatever it costs, do
  not keep them.* As George leaves the Time Room, a **hooded Unspeakable** he does
  not know presses Fred's tool into his hands and is gone before he can ask a name.
- **HX-01 → HX-03.** George recovers Remus, Pettigrew, Snape — each pure, each
  helping. Rifts recur; the war worsens. (From the moment he takes Fred's tool,
  one question haunts him — *could I use this on Fred?* — a slow burn that crests
  at the midpoint.)
- **Midpoint — the Temptation (see §8).**
- **HX-04 — second meeting with Dumbledore.** Up close: ideal Dumbledore vs. the
  directive-wraiths. George understands the dam, and the price.
- **HX-05 → HX-06.** Sirius, then Bellatrix (at peace, wanting out). The last
  pure soul sealed.
- **Finale — reaching Dumbledore (see §8).**

## 8. The two branches

### Midpoint — the Temptation (player choice)
The directive **cannot make George destroy** — he is no host. So it does the only
thing left: it **offers.** Through a rift it wears **Fred's voice** and offers to
turn the Sanctuary **two-way** — just once, just for Fred. The villain weaponizes
*love* where it could not use hate. **Trigger:** right after **HX-03** (Snape —
"nothing is what it seems"), when the dam first cracks; it has haunted George
since he took the tool at HX-01.

- **If the player refuses:** the story continues.
- **If the player accepts (opt-in):** George turns the tool on his own grief —
  and in doing so abandons the seals. Dumbledore's dam breaks, the directive
  floods into the unsealed, and the world burns. **George and Fred reunite … as
  the world we know ends. The CTF stops here.** (Implementation note: present as
  a clearly-marked, deliberate choice; the bad-end is a narrative branch, not
  silent destruction of progress — decide in build whether it's a hard stop or a
  "step back from the brink".)

### Finale — Dumbledore's fate (capstone challenge)
To **save** Dumbledore, George must pass a **final quiz drawn from all six
challenges** — a capstone that recombines what every stage taught (clues,
identifiers, indicators, the investigation code).

- **Pass — "The Office, Again" (the circular ending).** Dumbledore is pulled out
  **whole.** The six are at rest; the rifts fall quiet; the worn place in time
  begins to heal — *because George has finally stopped pulling at it.* Then the
  ending folds back to where it began:
  - **(The reveal)** The hooded Unspeakable who armed him at the start steps
    forward and lowers the hood: **Fred** — the one who lived, from the branch
    where George fell, the maker of the mercy George has carried the whole way.
    George turns the tool over and finally *sees* it — `GRED & FORGE` — and
    understands he has been holding his brother's hand since the first fragment.
    **He meets Fred again.**
  - **(C)** The **saved** Dumbledore steps *into* the old memory to hand it back —
    mirroring the prologue, where memory-Dumbledore reached *out* across time. The
    man who reached forward to start the war reaches back to end it.
  - **(A)** Not a stolen loop this time but a **farewell granted.** George steps
    into the office — warm light, McGonagall's voice fading, **his own Fred**
    beside him (the dead one; the living one stands at his shoulder — he cannot
    have the brother he lost back, but he is no longer alone in the losing).
    Nothing branches; nothing is wrong. He says the thing he never got to say, and
    they trade the codename one last time — *"Gred." "Forge."*
  - **(B)** And the prank that McGonagall cut short **finally lands** — the
    punchline the twins never got to see together, Peeves and all, the laugh they
    never finished.
  - Then **George closes the loop himself**, gently — the exact inverse of the
    prologue, where Dumbledore forced it shut to protect him. **Fawkes** takes
    wing back into the memory, to his own time. Last frame: the office, still and
    warm — **the opening scene** — now a place George can finally *leave.*
- **Fail:** Dumbledore **sacrifices this version of himself again** — the
  warning-giver gives the last thing he has. And there is no office to return to:
  the loop goes **dark**, not warm. George doesn't get it back. *(That contrast —
  the warm office vs. the dark loop — is what makes the capstone matter.)*

*(CTFd mapping: a 7th "capstone" challenge, unlocked after HX-06, whose questions
synthesize the prior handovers. Pass → good-end flag → the circular ending.)*

## 9. Themes

- **Grief and release.** Every mechanic is about whether you can let the dead go.
- **Mercy as the hard choice.** Sealing is not defeat of enemies; it's funeral rites.
- **The cost of clinging.** George's gift (time) is also his wound; the tool is
  both. The temptation makes the theme a *choice*, not a lecture.
- **The dead are not their worst moment.** Snape, Pettigrew, Bellatrix — pure,
  given one last chance to help. The villain is a directive, not a person.

## 11. Story ⇄ CTFd mapping (keep them in sync)

- **Stage = fragment.** Six challenges, sealed in order via Prerequisites.
- **Handover = the ghost's aid.** The clue each stage passes forward is the dead
  helping George (reframe of the existing integration handovers).
- **Hints = "a friend's whisper"** (the 5% penalty = the cost of leaning on them).
- **Flag `HORCRUX{…}` = the sealed fragment.** Submitting it to CTFd *is* casting
  the Sanctuary — checked server-side, never in the frontend. **Exception: HX-04.**
  Dumbledore is the dam and is *not* sealed there; its flag is the **evidence he
  surfaces**, and he is saved/released only in the capstone finale (§8).
- **Temporal rifts = interstitials** between stages in the theme.
- **Capstone quiz = a 7th challenge** after HX-06; its ending branches on pass/fail.
- **Temptation = a narrative branch** in the frontend at the midpoint.

## 12. Writing conventions (for the scene drafts)

- **Two cuts per stage.** A **cinematic beat** — second person (*"The map warms
  under your hand"*) — for the in-theme interstitial the player sees on
  unlock/solve, and a **fuller prose** cut — third-limited past, on George — kept
  in `story/` for the companion writeup. Same beats, two registers.
- **Each scene carries:** the ghost's greeting, how it *deliberately* aids George,
  the recognition beat, and the handover line that opens the next stage.
- **A little dialogue, sparingly.** Each scene gets a few spoken lines — a short
  exchange between George and the ghost — never a full conversation; the weight
  stays in the narration.
