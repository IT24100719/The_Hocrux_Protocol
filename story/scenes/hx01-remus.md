## Cinematic cut (second person — in-theme interstitials)

**[ON UNLOCK — shown when HX-01 opens]**

Dumbledore's warning still rings — *the dead you never finished grieving will
return wearing their own faces; do not keep them.* Then the hooded Unspeakable in
the corridor, pressing a thing into your hands and gone before you could ask a
name. It rides cold against your hip now, and it feels *familiar* — the weight,
the make, as if you've held it a thousand times — and you cannot think why. The
not-knowing itches.

Fawkes settles on the chair-back, a phoenix out of his own time, keeping company
with a man half out of his. On the Network, one file has woken: an old map of the
castle, drawn in ink that *moves.* A note rides it — *metadata is only the
doormat.* Moony always did say the interesting things are never where you're told
to look. Don't read the label. **Read past the surface. Read the blue.**

**[ON SOLVE — shown when the flag is accepted]**

The faint ink resolves out of the noise like a Patronus out of fog — not
coordinates, a *message*, laid one bit at a time into the quietest part of the
picture, where only a friend who'd learned to look underneath would find it. A
fragment: **mischief managed.**

And a hand, reaching forward. Remus Lupin — whole, threadbare, kind, the weapon
cut out of him somewhere up the line.

*"Hello, George,"* he says, the voice exactly as you kept it — tired, gentle.
*"You found the doormat was a lie. Good lad. I left the rest where only a friend
would think to look."*

*"Moony."* It's all you have. He died the night you lost Fred; you never let
yourself grieve him.

You raise the tool. *"Mischief managed,"* you say — the old words, the ones that
put the map to sleep — and you let him go. Not away. Home. The ink stills.

*"There's a door after this,"* he says, already fading into the light. *"A
chamber. Mind yourself — the one who waits there wore other men's faces for a
living. Nothing in that place is what it says it is."* And in the last of the ink
he leaves you a road: the portal, and a name the place has never stopped
remembering — **triddle.**

## Prose cut (third-limited past — companion writeup)

The name Dumbledore had given him was still warm when the first file woke.

George had not slept. He sat in the Time Room with Fawkes on the chair-back — the
Time-Room Fawkes, the one who had felt it first and followed him out of a memory —
and watched the Network the way other men watched the sea. Most of it lay quiet.
One thing did not: an old map of Hogwarts had surfaced in the archive, a thing of
moving ink and folding corridors, and it was pulsing like something with a
heartbeat.

The tool sat cold against his hip. A hooded Unspeakable had pressed it into his
hands as he left the Department — no name, no word, gone down the corridor before
George could call after him. It should have meant nothing to him. Instead it sat
in his palm like something he had held a thousand times, familiar down to its
weight and its make, and he could not for his life say why. The not-knowing had
followed him all the way here.

The map carried a note, the way these things did. *Metadata is only the doormat,*
it read; *the secret is woven into the picture.* George almost smiled. It was such
a Moony thing to write — the quiet one, the one who had taught a generation that
the interesting truths were never where you were pointed. The doormat was a
decoy. The message lay underneath the colour itself, in the least of the blue,
bit by patient bit, set down for someone who had learned to look past what a
surface wanted him to see.

So he looked. And it resolved out of the noise like a Patronus out of fog:
*mischief managed* — three words of sense where there should have been none, and
then more, a direction, a door.

And Remus. Not a wraith, not a weapon — Remus Lupin himself, threadbare and kind
and entirely whole, the directive cleaved away from him by a hand further up the
line. He had died the same night Fred had. George had buried that grief with all
the others he had never allowed himself to open.

"Hello, George," Remus said, and the voice was exactly as George had kept it —
tired, and kind, and unsurprised. "You found the doormat was a lie. I hid the
rest where only a friend would think to look."

"Moony." It was all he had.

"Mischief managed," George said then — the old words, the ones that put the map
to sleep — and he turned the tool on the light and let Remus go. Not away. Home.
The ink stilled. For the first time in a long while, something ended gently.

"There is a door after this one," Remus said, already thinning into the light.
"A chamber. Mind yourself, George — the one who waits there wore other men's
faces for a living. Nothing in that place will be what it says it is." And in the
last of the ink he left a road: the portal, and a name it had never stopped
remembering — *triddle.*
