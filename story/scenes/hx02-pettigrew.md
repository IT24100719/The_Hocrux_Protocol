## Prose cut (third-limited past — companion writeup)

The portal was almost insulting in its plainness — a name, a word, a button that
said *Enter* — after a map that moved and a dead man's voice. George tried the
honest way first, because some reflex in him still did, and the Chamber turned him
out without apology. The researcher whose name Moony had handed him — *triddle* —
had left no password a living stranger could guess.

But the door was unlatched. He could feel it the way he could feel the rifts now,
the way he could feel the tool at his hip that he still could not account for: a
give in the works, a place where the directive had forced something open and
never closed it. And in the dark behind the give, something small and patient
waited, the way a rat waits in a wall.

"They never check the face, George." The voice was thin, and apologetic, and it
turned his stomach before he placed it. "Only the name. Give them the name — and
then tell the lock the question's finished, so it never thinks to ask for the word
that would prove you're him."

So George wore another man's name past the glass, and the lock, satisfied by the
name alone, forgot to ask for the rest. He was through. *Diary of triddle* — and
the pages gave up what the place had been keeping: a fragment that said *the diary
remembers*, and beneath it a message in someone else's hand, scrambled, waiting
for a key it did not carry.

And there he was. Small and grey and flinching from his own helpfulness — Peter
Pettigrew, Wormtail, the directive cut out of him somewhere up the line, wringing
hands that had spent half a life as paws.

"Twelve years you slept in my mother's house," George said. "As the family rat."

"I know." Pettigrew would not look at him. "I was good at being what I wasn't. It
was the only thing I was ever any good at." His hands twisted. "Let it be good for
something, George. Just once."

And George understood the shape of the gift: the man who had betrayed everyone who
ever trusted him was, at the last, betraying the one man who had never deserved
trust at all — handing a thief the key to his master's diary and calling it
penance. George had every reason in the world to hate him. He was surprised, and
a little ashamed, to find that what he felt instead was pity. He raised the tool,
and let Wormtail go, and even that small mercy seemed to catch the man off guard,
as though he had not expected to be included in anyone's gentleness ever again.

The scrambled message stayed behind, patient as a held breath. "The next one hid
the truth in runes," Pettigrew whispered, thinning into nothing. "The key is a
name this place keeps whispering. You already know it." And then he was gone, and
for the first time George did not watch a ghost leave and think of Fred.
