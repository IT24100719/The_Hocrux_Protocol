## Prose cut (third-limited past — companion writeup)

The message had come out of the diary as nonsense, and George had learned by now
not to trust a surface. Wormtail had sworn he already held the key, and he did —
the name the Chamber had never stopped whispering, the name everything in this
place seemed keyed to. *Riddle.* He turned it through the runes the way you work a
key you were handed in the dark, and one shift at a time the wall of letters gave
up pretending.

A name fell out of it: **USER-472** — an account to be pulled from the restricted
archive, a trail running outward from it, and, at the end, the fragment, plain as
a confession: *classical runes broken.*

Snape was watching before George thought to look for him. Not threadbare and kind
like Moony, not grey and cringing like the rat — exact, austere, contained, a man
who had made a life's art of giving nothing away, and resented, even now, being
seen doing a kindness.

"I made it breakable," he said, before George could frame the question. "In life
I could never permit myself to be read. Consider this the correction."

"The key was Riddle," George said.

"Everything I was, was keyed to him." There was no pride in it and no shame;
Snape stated it the way he might have stated a property of a potion. "It is the
only key that ever fit." And then, with an edge that was almost concern and would
have died rather than admit it: "Guard your mind, Weasley. The thing up the line
cannot make you burn — you are no one's host. So it will not come at you through
what you fear. It will come through what you love. When it wears a voice you would
give your years to hear again, you will want to answer it. Do not."

Behind him the air split. George had seen the rifts at a distance the whole way —
Dumbledore's war, glimpsed — but this one opened close, and widening, and out of
it came a sound he had spent a decade teaching himself not to listen for. It was
laughing. It was Fred.

George raised the tool while his hand still obeyed him. Snape, who had asked for
nothing, inclined his head once — permission, or thanks, or neither — and let
himself be read, and let himself go.

"The account you want," he said, already going, "answered to the man in the
archive. You will know him when you find him. Everyone always did." And then the
cold, exact presence was gone, and the rift did not close, and the laughing did
not stop.
