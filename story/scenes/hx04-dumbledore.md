## Prose cut (third-limited past — companion writeup)

Snape had promised he would know the man in the archive, and Snape, it turned out,
had not been speaking lightly.

The Restricted Section had become a wall of shelves gone to static — records
flickering like a signal under attack, because that was precisely what they were.
Something was fighting for the archive, or in it. And yet the records George
needed had been *kept* — held legible by some patient hand working in the gaps
between blows. An account sat flagged where he could not miss it: USER-472, its
traffic scattered through the access log and the system log both, waiting to be
drawn together. An outbound trail ran from it, laid almost helpfully bare. And an
evidence capture had been left with its metadata unscrubbed — pointedly, the way a
man leaves a door unlatched for someone he is expecting.

George pulled the account and crossed the logs, and the story closed around a
single minute: 23:47:16, an outbound connection to 10.10.20.45 on port 4444,
egress where the baseline swore there should be none. The fragment itself was
tucked into the image's metadata like a note slipped into a returned library
book: *restricted section logs.*

Then the static parted, and he saw the war he had only ever caught in the edges of
the rifts.

There was not one Albus Dumbledore. There were a dozen — the best of him, the
truest, the versions a man spends a life trying and failing to be — and they stood
in a line against shapes that wore stolen faces. Some of the shapes wore his own.
Two wore faces George had not yet reached. All of it strained against a single
will that was, visibly, losing. Dumbledore was ravaged down to something George
had never seen in him and had not known he feared to see. And he was, under all of
it, glad.

"Hello again, George," he said — and he said it the way he had said it across the
desk in the prologue, with McGonagall half in the doorway and Fred still laughing
at George's shoulder, as though this were that afternoon and not a battlefield at
the end of everything. "You let your brother go. I know what that cost you. I am
sorrier than I can say that it had to be asked."

Three of the shapes were guttering out as George watched — Moony, the rat, the
cold man — the wraiths he had lifted away, one for each soul carried home. "You
have taken three from me already," Dumbledore said, and something eased in him as
each one failed. "I feel the weight of every one you set down. Do not imagine it
goes unnoticed."

George raised the tool — the reflex of three stages, the mercy he had learned to
give — and Dumbledore stopped him with a look older than the castle.

"Not me. Not yet." Gentle, and absolute. "If I fall while the others still wake,
there is nothing left between the world and the fire but you, and you are not yet
enough alone. Hold your hand, George. There is an order to this, and I am the end
of it."

He turned George toward the next door, the indicator warm and terrible in his
hands. "A friend is waiting in the fire. Take him what you have found. Then the
vault — you will not like who keeps it, and you will be wrong about her. And
then," he said, and the smile he managed was the smallest and the gravest George
had ever had from him, "come back for me. If you can."

So George went, because he was told to, and because he understood now that staying
would only spend the old man faster. It was the hardest door he had walked through
yet, and he did not look back, because he did not trust himself to keep walking if
he did.
