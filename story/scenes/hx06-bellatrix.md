## Prose cut (third-limited past — companion writeup)

The fire's last word had been a door. A vault at 10.10.20.60, goblin-made, the
sort of thing the old world had built to be unbreakable and had mostly succeeded.
George carried what the flames had handed him: a servant's name to wear at the
threshold, and a code to speak — *GRINGOTTS-472* — and those two things together
let him into the outer dark of it, no further.

The last fragment was kept deeper, where only the vault's master was ever meant to
reach, behind work no honest thief had beaten in a thousand years. George was not
going to beat it honestly. There never was an unbreakable thing; there was only a
flaw not yet found, and this one had been left almost fondly in the open — a
single command the vault had been told it was safe to trust, trusted with far too
much. He took it, and climbed it, and came up at the root of the whole vault with
the thing owned end to end. The door that no one beat swung for him. *Goblin made
vault owned.* And at the bottom of it waited the last fragment, and the last of
them.

He braced for the monster. He had spent his whole life knowing exactly what
Bellatrix Lestrange was. Dumbledore had told him he would be wrong, and Dumbledore,
as usual, was.

"The flaw was mine," she said. There was none of the old sing-song in it, none of
the fever — only something level, and tired, and almost dry. "I left it for you. A
lock that wants to be picked. Imagine that, after everything."

"My mother killed you," George said, because he could not stand in front of her
and not say it.

"I know whose son you are." She looked at him without heat, without the hunger he
remembered from every story he'd ever heard of her. "Good. She did it for her
daughter. I was killed by a mother protecting her child — there are a great many
worse ways to go, and I have handed out most of them." For a moment the old edge
surfaced and then thought better of itself. "I would have given anything to have
been made of something that ordinary. Instead I was made of *him.* Every corner
of me, for as far back as I can find. Do you know what that hollows out of a
person, to belong so completely to someone who never once belonged to you?"

Her cousin's name sat unspoken in the room between them. George had freed Sirius
scarcely an hour ago, and she knew it; of course she knew it.

"Did he run?" she asked.

"Yes," George said.

Something almost soft moved across that terrible face. "He always had somewhere he
would rather be. I envied him that more than I ever hated him for it — and I hated
him a great deal. I never had an elsewhere. I only ever had *him.*"

Then she straightened, and what came next was the fiercest thing George had heard
in any of these rooms, fiercer than the war, fiercer than the fire. "I will not be
his leftover. Not his weapon. Not his monument. Not the last clever thing he
thought to leave behind when the rest of him burned. Take me out, Weasley." It was
not a plea. Bellatrix Lestrange did not plead. It was a demand, the last one she
had. "Let me not be his, at the end."

So George did. He gave Bellatrix Lestrange the one thing no living soul would ever
have thought to give her — *out* — and she took it the way she had taken
everything, without apology and without a backward look, and for the first time in
the whole long accounting of her she belonged to no one at all.

That made five. The dam had almost nothing left to hold. *Go and get the old man,*
the silence after her said. *He is the only one of us who ever chose this. See
that he is let, at last, to stop.*
