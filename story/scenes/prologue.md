## Cinematic cut (second person — intro sequence)

**[I · The Time Room]**

You are not supposed to be here — not at this hour, not with the hourglass turning
for a reason the Department never sanctioned. But grief makes its own rules, and
yours have brought you back to the Time Room every night for longer than you will
admit, because somewhere in its turning is one day you refuse to put down. You have
worn yourself thin against it. Thin enough that time, lately, has begun to show you
its seams.

**[II · The memory]**

The loop settles, and it is the good one: you and Fred, sixteen and incorrigible,
marched up to the Headmaster's office — McGonagall still furious about the corridor
you and Peeves rigged so Filch kept reliving the same ten seconds. You'd signed it,
naturally. *Gred & Forge.* Dumbledore is trying not to laugh. Fred is at your
shoulder, alive, mid-grin.

You could live here. You have been trying to.

**[III · The branch]**

Then Fawkes lifts his head.

The phoenix has gone rigid on his perch, staring at nothing and everything, and the
warmth goes out of the room like a held breath. Something is waking in the present —
something that was his master's — and the memory feels it through you. The loop
shudders. And Dumbledore, who cannot possibly see you, turns and looks you dead in
the eye across twenty years.

*"George,"* he says — and it is not a boy's memory of the voice; it is older,
graver, aimed. *"Listen, while there is time to."*

**[IV · The warning]**

*"The boy who became a dark lord never trusted death to keep him. He left the world
one last gift, and it is waking — six of them, wearing faces you will know. The
dead you never finished grieving are going to come back to you, and they will need
you to let them go."* His eyes do not leave yours. *"Do not keep them, George.
Whatever it costs. Promise me you will not keep them."*

A map rises unbidden behind your eyes — old parchment, moving ink, a castle you'd
know blind. *"Start there,"* he says. *"And mind the surface. It always lies."*

Then, gently, the way you shut a door on a sleeping house, he forces the loop
closed — to put you out of the path of what is coming.

**[V · The tool]**

You come back to yourself with the hourglass still and Fawkes — *this* Fawkes,
somehow, the one from the loop — settling onto the back of your chair as though he
came through with you.

In the corridor a figure is waiting: hooded, nameless, Unspeakable as the rest of
them, and gone before you can speak — but not before pressing something into your
hands. A small instrument, cold and intricate. It lifts souls out of time; you know
that the way you know your own name, though no one has told you. And it feels
*familiar*, the weight and the make of it, as though you have held it a thousand
times. You cannot think why.

On the Network, a file has just woken. An old map of the castle, drawn in ink that
moves.

Dark times are here. *Go.*

## Prose cut (third-limited past — companion writeup)

George Weasley was not supposed to be in the Time Room at that hour, and the
hourglass was not supposed to be turning, and the day he had it turned to was not
one the Department of Mysteries would ever have signed off on. But that was rather
the point of grief, he had found: it wrote its own rules, and his had led him back
to this room every night for years, because somewhere in its slow turning was a
single afternoon he had never been able to set down.

He had worn himself thin against it. Thin enough that time had begun, lately, to
show him its seams — rifts at the edge of his vision, things half-glimpsed that no
one else could see. He had stopped reporting them. They were, if he was honest, the
only company he kept.

The loop settled into the good one. Not the second of May — never that one. This
one was warm: he and Fred, sixteen and impossible, being marched up to the
Headmaster's office with McGonagall three steps behind and incandescent, because
they had talked Peeves into rigging a corridor so that anyone who crossed it lived
the same ten seconds over and over, and Filch had been stamping the same three
steps for the better part of an hour before anyone thought to fetch a teacher. They
had signed it, of course. They signed everything. *Gred & Forge.*

In the memory Dumbledore was fighting a losing battle with his own composure, and
Fred was at George's shoulder, alive and mid-grin, and George thought, as he always
thought here, that he could live in this. That he had, more or less, been trying
to.

Then Fawkes lifted his head.

The phoenix had gone rigid on his perch, and the warmth went out of the room like a
breath held too long. Something was waking in the present — something that had been
the old man's, once — and the recording felt it through George the way a pond feels
a stone. The loop shuddered. And Albus Dumbledore, who had been twenty years dead
and could not possibly have known George was watching, turned from the boys at his
desk and looked George dead in the eye across all of it.

"George," he said. It was not the warm, amused voice of the memory. It was older
than that, and graver, and it was aimed. "Listen, while there is time to."

And he told him. That the boy who had made himself a dark lord had never once
trusted death to keep him; that he had left the world a last gift, carved in secret
into six who had not asked for it, and that the gift was waking now, wearing faces
George would know. "The dead you never finished grieving are going to come back to
you," Dumbledore said, "and they are going to need you to let them go." His eyes did
not waver. "Do not keep them, George. Whatever it costs you. Promise me that you
will not keep them."

A map had risen behind George's eyes as the old man spoke — old parchment, moving
ink, a castle he could have walked blind. "Start there," Dumbledore said. "And mind
the surface. It always lies." And then, gently, the way a man eases shut the door of
a house where someone is sleeping, he closed the loop — pushing George out of it, and
out of the path of whatever was coming.

George came back to himself in the Time Room with the hourglass gone still and a
phoenix settling onto the back of his chair — this Fawkes, impossibly, the one from
inside the memory, as though he had followed George out through the seam.

There was a figure waiting in the corridor. Hooded, and nameless, and Unspeakable as
any of them, and gone down the passage before George could get a word out — but not
before pressing something into his hands. It was small, and cold, and intricate, and
George understood what it did the moment he closed his fingers on it, the way you
understand your own name: it lifted souls out of time. No one had told him that. No
one had needed to. And it sat in his palm as though he had held it ten thousand
times, familiar down to its weight and its make, and he could not, for the life of
him, think why.

Somewhere on the Network, a file had just woken. An old map of the castle, drawn in
ink that moved.

The dark times the old man had promised were not coming. They were here. George put
the strange familiar instrument in his pocket, and he went to meet them.
